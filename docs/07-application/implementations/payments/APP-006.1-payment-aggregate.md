# Aggregate Architecture

```text
Payment

    │

    ├── Reservation Snapshot

    ├── Quote Snapshot

    ├── Pricing Snapshot

    ├── Payment Method

    ├── Authorization

    ├── Capture

    ├── Settlement

    ├── Refunds

    ├── Timeline

    └── Metadata
```

The aggregate owns immutable copies of every commercial settlement artefact.

Payment is a distinct Financial Aggregate representing one individual transaction. Reservation owns its own lifecycle and historical PaymentSnapshot and is persisted separately. Payment does not calculate the commercial initial-payment requirement, and an individual Payment does not establish full Reservation settlement. There is no parallel Payment Aggregate or financial source of truth.

The canonical Payment retains its domain Reservation reference and immutable Reservation snapshot. Its persisted association to the canonical Reservation ID is restrictive; the reference, snapshot and association must agree. Neither one-to-one Payment–Reservation cardinality nor PaymentAttempt cardinality is implied. Required Booking and legacy handling remain unresolved as described by proposed SPEC-027.

# Factory Constructors

Provide:

```typescript
Payment.create(...)
```

Creates a new immutable aggregate.

---

Provide:

```typescript
Payment.restore(...)
```

Restores an immutable aggregate from persistence.

No validation logic beyond invariant enforcement.

**Proposed persistence alignment:** restoration must receive complete, validated canonical state from the Payment repository, including all owned refunds and historical snapshots, without recalculation or fabricated lifecycle records. Missing, invalid or unmappable data must be reported rather than silently restored as a complete Aggregate. Proposed SPEC-027 §20.1 defines the required reconstruction inventory and unresolved mappings; proposed SPEC-029 §7.1 defines the conditional persistence boundary.

All monetary members, including stage records, refunds and Pricing snapshot values, must round-trip exactly under the approved decimal and currency contract. The existing application `number` types cannot guarantee this; the application representation and affected APP-004.2/Reservation precision contract require separate owner approval before implementation. This draft does not choose a replacement API type.

The narrow persistence-neutral `PaymentRepository` interface belongs in `src/application/payments` and is implemented in Infrastructure; no Prisma types cross that boundary. The conditional-save contract remains subject to SPEC-029 approval. No Aggregate is moved into Domain.

# Lifecycle

Canonical payment lifecycle:

```text
Created

↓

PendingAuthorization

↓

Authorized

↓

Captured

↓

Settled

↓

Completed
```

Alternative flows:

```text
Created

↓

AuthorizationFailed

↓

Cancelled
```

```text
Captured

↓

RefundRequested

↓

Refunded
```

Future stages such as PartialCapture, PartialRefund and Chargeback may extend the lifecycle without changing the aggregate contract.

**Proposed D05 evidence gate:** verified provider evidence and canonical lifecycle status are separate. A verified PayFast `COMPLETE` must be preserved as evidence without changing the canonical Payment status or creating authorisation, capture, settlement, events, amounts or timestamps. No Reservation is automatically confirmed from that notification while the canonical transition is unresolved. A separate provider-neutral transition must first be explicitly approved with its meaning, admissible evidence, resulting status, event and timestamp semantics and the corresponding PayFast mapping. Evidence persistence details remain unapproved; this proposal creates no new lifecycle stage.

# Milestone Outcome

Upon successful completion, GCT Core shall expose a provider-independent immutable Payment Aggregate that represents the canonical commercial settlement record of the platform.

The aggregate establishes the foundation of the Payments Capability by owning immutable payment state while remaining independent of processing, presentation and provider implementations.
