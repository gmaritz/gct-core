## 3. Current architectural position

**Existing authority and observed path** — see [AR-003.3 §§2-3](AR-003.3-payment-aggregate-persistence-and-itn-reconciliation-design.md#2-evidence-and-authority-baseline) and [SPEC-002 §8.10](../../../06-specifications/engineering/SPEC-002-canonical-physical-data-model.md#810-application-owned-journey-workflow-state):

- Reservation is the canonical Commercial Aggregate Root and owns its lifecycle and historical PaymentSnapshot. Payment is a separate Financial Aggregate Root and is authoritative for payment status and financial outcomes. PaymentAttempt is a separate Reservation-linked record of an individual attempt, not a replacement Payment.
- A verified ITN is the authoritative external **notification** path for asynchronous PayFast financial-state changes; a provider status query may also provide evidence where required. Browser return and cancel callbacks are read-only and do not establish financial success. The PayFast specification states this contract but does not explicitly declare itself Approved; its identifier and status need confirmation.
- Application orchestration coordinates Payment outcomes with Reservation confirmation. [SPEC-029 §§21 and 29](../../../06-specifications/engineering/SPEC-029-repository-persistence-architecture%20v2.0.md#21-payment-snapshot) prohibit Reservation persistence from modifying Payment or fetching live payment status and exclude unrelated Aggregate Roots and provider calls from the Reservation repository transaction.
- The observed ITN path updates PaymentAttempt and PaymentSnapshot, but no persisted Payment Aggregate update or canonical Reservation confirmation coordinator was identified. D07 approves separate local transaction boundaries and D08 approves a recovery direction; their detailed contracts and implementation remain outstanding.
- This review concerns the existing Reservation. It does not propose a workflow-based or replacement Reservation, a Session Grant or workflow-state persistence as a payment workaround.

### D01 — Canonical Payment identity?

- **Question:** Which identity should the canonical Payment use for the existing initial flow?
- **Evidence and constraint:** The resolver derives `payment-${reservationId}`; a derived string alone is not a persisted Payment. `Payment.id` is the canonical Payment Aggregate identity, while Prisma `Payment.paymentReference` is a separate unique business-reference field. Neither is the `PaymentAttempt.paymentReference` sent as PayFast `m_payment_id`. No general one-Payment-per-Reservation rule is established. [AR-003.3 §4](AR-003.3-payment-aggregate-persistence-and-itn-reconciliation-design.md#4-identity-and-relationship-mapping)
- **Architect recommendation:** Option A — retain `payment-${reservationId}` for the existing Reservation's scoped first full-payment flow as canonical `Payment.id`. For newly created canonical records, propose the same value for the separate `Payment.paymentReference` business-reference field, unless review establishes a different existing contract. Keep `PaymentAttempt.paymentReference`, sent as `m_payment_id`, separate. Specification-owner confirmation remains outstanding.
- **Alternative:** Generate a new unrelated ID.
- **Consequences:** Stable retry identity requires collision and consumer review, preserving the existing unique business-reference column and reviewing compatibility with its legacy values; it does not decide future Payment cardinality or attempt identity.
- **Project Owner decision:** **APPROVED — Option A**, 2026-11-17.
- **Conditions/comments:** "Agree, subject to collision, legacy-reference and consumer compatibility checks." These checks remain outstanding. This approval does not establish a general one-Payment-per-Reservation rule or determine PaymentAttempt cardinality.
- **Confirmation:** SPEC-026 identity confirmation; SPEC-027/028 identity and migration review; SPEC-029 repository contract review.

### D02 — How does Payment reference Reservation?

- **Question:** How should Payment identify the existing Reservation without treating Booking as its owner?
- **Evidence and constraint:** The existing physical Payment is Booking-linked and lacks a direct Reservation association; the canonical Payment includes Reservation reference and snapshot. [AR-003.3 §§4, 6.2](AR-003.3-payment-aggregate-persistence-and-itn-reconciliation-design.md#62-mapping-assessment)
- **Architect recommendation:** Option A — add an explicit restrictive Reservation reference and preserve the immutable snapshot; verify they agree, with no inferred one-to-one relationship.
- **Alternatives:** Infer Reservation from Booking; retain only the snapshot.
- **Consequences:** Supports a Reservation without Booking, but requires approved nullable legacy treatment and relationship/constraint changes.
- **Project Owner decision:** **APPROVED IN PRINCIPLE — Option A**, 2026-11-17. The persisted canonical Payment Aggregate must have an explicit, restrictive association with the canonical Reservation Aggregate using the canonical Reservation ID, while retaining the existing domain-level Reservation reference and immutable Reservation snapshot capabilities.
- **Conditions:** Outstanding compatibility review of the existing mandatory Booking relationship; legacy Payment record and reference treatment; and consistency enforcement between the Payment Reservation reference, immutable snapshot and persisted association. This decision establishes neither a one-to-one Payment–Reservation relationship nor PaymentAttempt cardinality. It does not authorise implementation.
- **Confirmation:** Required specification-owner and architecture-governance confirmations remain outstanding, including relevant SPEC-026–029 reviews: SPEC-026 relationship clarification; SPEC-027/028 mapping; and SPEC-029 review and confirmation of Aggregate boundaries.

### D03 — How is the full Aggregate represented?

- **Question:** How should existing Payment and Refund persistence represent and reconstruct the complete canonical Payment Aggregate?
- **Evidence and constraint:** Current Payment and Refund models cannot demonstrate a full Aggregate round trip. SPEC-028 §30 restricts JSON to structures defined by SPEC-027; the PayFast specification bars a parallel Payment persistence architecture and does not approve these particular schema changes. [AR-003.3 §§6.2-6.6](AR-003.3-payment-aggregate-persistence-and-itn-reconciliation-design.md#63-aggregate-to-physical-mapping-matrix)
- **Architect recommendation:** Option A — extend the existing Payment and Refund representations, retain one source of truth per financial fact, and approve a precision-safe money contract and any JSON representation before use.
- **Alternatives:** Leave the models unchanged, which the evidence does not establish as sufficient; create a parallel table, which risks a conflicting parallel persistence architecture and is outside this review's authorisation.
- **Consequences:** Requires physical-model and migration approval, legacy compatibility, explicit Refund mapping, nullable Booking/payment-date assessment, exact decimal reconstruction and a safe rollback plan.
- **Project Owner decision:** **APPROVED — Recommended Option A**, 2026-11-19. Extend the existing Payment and Refund persistence representations to support complete canonical Aggregate reconstruction; do not introduce a parallel Payment table or competing financial source of truth.
- **Conditions:** Define a precision-safe monetary persistence and reconstruction contract; explicitly map Refund information; assess the existing mandatory Booking relationship and nullable legacy treatment; and verify compatibility with existing Payment records and references. Obtain the required SPEC-027, SPEC-028 and SPEC-029 approvals and approve the migration and rollback strategy before implementation.
- **Confirmation:** SPEC-027/028 amendments including JSON; SPEC-029 mapping; APP-006.1/006.2 and APP-004.2 precision contracts; other affected owners as identified in AR-003.3. This decision does not authorise implementation.

### D04 — Where and how is PaymentRepository defined?

- **Question:** Which architectural layer owns the persistence-neutral Payment repository contract?
- **Evidence and constraint:** APP-006.1 locates Payment in the application layer, while SPEC-001 places repository interfaces in the domain layer. A domain interface must not import application types. [AR-003.3 §6.1](AR-003.3-payment-aggregate-persistence-and-itn-reconciliation-design.md#61-repository-contract-and-layering)
- **Architect recommendation:** Option A — retain the established Aggregate location with a narrowly scoped persistence-neutral application interface, subject to formal reconciliation of the layering decision and affected authority.
- **Alternative:** Move canonical contracts inward without creating a second Payment Aggregate.
- **Consequences:** Requires an approved dependency direction and conditional-save contract; the decision does not itself amend specifications or approve a detailed repository API.
- **Project Owner decision:** **APPROVED — Option A**, 2026-11-17. Retain the canonical Payment Aggregate in its established Application Layer location, `src/application/payments`. Define a narrowly scoped, persistence-neutral `PaymentRepository` interface in the Application Layer, implemented by Infrastructure using the persistence technology. Do not move the canonical Payment Aggregate into the Domain Layer or create a second Payment Aggregate.
- **Conditions:** Formal reconciliation of SPEC-001's repository placement rule with APP-006.1; confirmation of dependency direction and interface ownership; definition and approval of the conditional-save contract through relevant specification review, including SPEC-029; and confirmation of consistency with SPEC-026–028. These remain outstanding. This decision does not authorise implementation.
- **Confirmation:** SPEC-001 and APP-006.1 alignment; SPEC-029 Payment repository clarification; SPEC-026/027/028 confirmation.

### D05 — How do lifecycle operations map verified provider outcomes?

- **Question:** What canonical Payment status may follow a verified PayFast `COMPLETE`, and what evidence supports each lifecycle stage?
- **Evidence and constraint:** The approved APP-006.1 lifecycle is staged; `COMPLETE` does not independently prove authorization, capture or settlement. Current status mapping does not establish a persisted Aggregate transition. [AR-003.3 §5.3](AR-003.3-payment-aggregate-persistence-and-itn-reconciliation-design.md#53-provider-outcome-canonical-status-and-evidence)
- **Architect recommendation:** Permit a provider-neutral direct completion transition only if its meaning, evidence, event and explicit lifecycle amendment are approved. Otherwise preserve verified evidence with canonical status unchanged and no automatic confirmation.
- **Alternatives:** Require independent evidence for every stage before completion; retain the outcome without automatic completion and resolve manually.
- **Consequences:** Requires lifecycle, evidence and event-contract decisions; never fabricate intermediate events, amounts or timestamps. Cancellation and failure mapping also remain open.
- **Project Owner decision:** **APPROVED — Conservative lifecycle option**, 2026-11-19. A verified PayFast `COMPLETE` notification must not automatically fabricate authorisation, capture or settlement events. Preserve verified provider evidence without advancing canonical Payment lifecycle status until an explicitly approved transition defines its meaning, acceptable evidence, resulting canonical status, associated domain event and timestamp semantics, and the corresponding PayFast integration contract.
- **Conditions:** Do not automatically confirm a Reservation from a provider notification while the required canonical Payment transition remains unresolved. The direct completion option remains unapproved unless separately and explicitly approved.
- **Confirmation:** APP-006.1/006.2 and PayFast specification amendments; APP-006 capability and SPEC-026/027/028 status mapping review; SPEC-029 confirmation.

### D06 — How are attempts associated and retried?

- **Question:** How should PaymentAttempt reference Payment, and how should initiation retries recover safely?
- **Evidence and constraint:** PaymentAttempt is Reservation-linked, but its `paymentId` is currently a string rather than a Payment foreign key. A persisted Payment without an attempt is not automatically invalid. [AR-003.3 §§4, 7.2](AR-003.3-payment-aggregate-persistence-and-itn-reconciliation-design.md#72-initiation-interruption-and-recovery-matrix)
- **Architect recommendation:** Require an existing Payment, add a Payment FK only after approval and legacy checks, verify Reservation equality, and use fenced same-key recovery. For idempotency and recovery, compare Payment identity, Reservation identity, provider, environment, exact amount and currency against the existing attempt and persisted Payment as applicable; compare payment method against the persisted Payment and requested context. Decide composite constraints and multiple-attempt policy separately.
- **Alternatives:** Application checks only; a composite Payment/Reservation FK rather than the proposed FK plus equality checks.
- **Consequences:** Prevents new attempts referencing nonexistent Payments; existing orphan attempts, Payment creation races and ambiguous form handoff require reconciliation. Terminal retries require a separately approved policy; no multiple-attempt policy or new cardinality constraint is assumed.
- **Project Owner decision:** **APPROVED — Recommended direction**, 2026-11-19. Require an existing canonical Payment before creating a PaymentAttempt. Approve an explicit Payment foreign-key association subject to legacy compatibility review, Reservation and Payment identity consistency checks, fenced same-key initiation recovery, and comparison of provider, environment, exact amount and currency against the existing attempt and persisted Payment as applicable.
- **Conditions:** Do not introduce a general one-to-one Payment–Reservation relationship or an unapproved PaymentAttempt cardinality constraint. Composite constraints, multiple-attempt policy and terminal retry behaviour remain subject to specification review; payment method comparison remains as described in the recommendation.
- **Confirmation:** SPEC-026 traceability clarification; SPEC-027/028 amendment; SPEC-029 ownership/transaction review and approved retry policy.

### D07 — What are ITN transaction boundaries?

- **Question:** How should provider correlation, Payment outcome, attempt outcome and Reservation updates be committed across Aggregate boundaries?
- **Evidence and constraint:** The current attempt transaction writes the Reservation snapshot but not Payment. SPEC-029 §29 excludes unrelated Aggregate Roots from the Reservation repository transaction. [AR-003.3 §§8.1-8.2](AR-003.3-payment-aggregate-persistence-and-itn-reconciliation-design.md#81-provider-verification-and-application-sequence)
- **Architect recommendation:** Separate local boundaries: Attempt correlation/evidence A1, Payment evidence/checkpoint P1, Attempt outcome A2, then Reservation R1; provider verification occurs outside transactions.
- **Alternative:** A cross-root transaction; it must not be assumed authorised and would require reconciliation with applicable authority.
- **Consequences:** A correlated provider ID is not a financial outcome; if P1 fails after A1, durable evidence and recovery are necessary. There is no single atomic cross-root success.
- **Project Owner decision:** **APPROVED — Separate local transaction boundaries**, 2026-11-19. Use A1 for Attempt correlation and durable evidence, P1 for Payment financial evidence and a reconciliation checkpoint, A2 for PaymentAttempt outcome, and R1 for Reservation snapshot and lifecycle update, in that order. Provider verification occurs outside database transactions.
- **Conditions:** Do not use a cross-Aggregate database transaction; each Aggregate retains its own persistence boundary. Recovery must be possible when a later boundary fails after an earlier boundary has committed.
- **Confirmation:** SPEC-026/027/028 traceability review; SPEC-029 orchestration and transaction-boundary clarification; ADR applicability under ADR-000.

### D08 — How are partial failures, duplicates and unmatched verified outcomes recovered?

- **Question:** What durable reconciliation and operational exception capability should recover verified outcomes, including notifications with no identifiable Payment?
- **Evidence and constraint:** A Payment checkpoint cannot represent an unknown Payment. Existing `FinancialReconciliation` is a batch summary; generic audit/event models have no demonstrated matching, retry and resolution contract. Provider redelivery cannot be assumed. [AR-003.3 §§8.2-8.5](AR-003.3-payment-aggregate-persistence-and-itn-reconciliation-design.md#85-verified-financial-exceptions---proposed-operational-boundary)
- **Architect recommendation:** Use monitored A1 and Payment-owned checkpoints for replay, plus a bounded Financial exception capability for unmatched verified evidence; reuse an existing capability only if its contract and authority are demonstrated.
- **Alternatives:** Rely on provider retries only; use a proven existing capability instead of a separately specified exception representation.
- **Consequences:** Owner must assign Financial/operations responsibility, retention, access, acknowledgement, retry, alerting, SLA and outage reconciliation. No unknown notification may be assigned to a guessed Payment or Reservation.
- **Project Owner decision:** **APPROVED — Recommended recovery direction**, 2026-11-19. Use monitored A1 correlation/evidence and Payment-owned checkpoints for replay and recovery, plus a bounded Financial exception capability for verified outcomes that cannot be matched to a known Payment. An unmatched notification must never be assigned to a guessed Payment or Reservation.
- **Conditions:** Before implementation, responsible owners must define Financial and operational ownership, access and retention, acknowledgement and resolution procedures, retry and alerting behaviour, recovery SLA and outage reconciliation. Reuse an existing capability only if its contract and authority are demonstrated; do not create an unnecessarily broad reconciliation platform.
- **Confirmation:** SPEC-026/027/028 Financial ownership and representation; SPEC-029 recovery contract; Financial, operations and security/privacy owners; ADR review where required.

### D09 — Who applies snapshot and confirmation, and under what policy?

- **Question:** Which Reservation operation applies an authoritative Payment outcome and when may it confirm the existing Reservation?
- **Evidence and constraint:** The canonical application Reservation has no identified confirmation method. The separate legacy method has a different lifecycle. Fully paid alone does not establish approved fulfilment or operational prerequisites. [AR-003.3 §§9-9.2](AR-003.3-payment-aggregate-persistence-and-itn-reconciliation-design.md#9-reservation-snapshot-and-confirmation-coordination)
- **Architect recommendation:** Use a provider-neutral application coordinator and approved immutable canonical Reservation operations, with exact-money conditional persistence and outcome provenance; retain Payment success if confirmation fails.
- **Alternative:** Reuse the incompatible legacy confirmation method, which AR-003.3 advises against without reconciliation.
- **Consequences:** B01 approves partial initial payment as a possible confirmation basis, subject to applicable conditions; it does not decide the confirmation operation, permitted predecessor states, detailed evidence and fulfilment conditions, multiple outcomes, cancellation or exception handling. Initial single-outcome provenance cannot safely represent multiple outcomes without further design.
- **Project Owner decision:** **APPROVED — Recommended provider-neutral coordination approach**, 2026-11-19. Use an application-level coordinator and approved canonical Reservation operations. Payment outcome processing remains separate from Reservation persistence; Reservation updates use exact-money conditional persistence and outcome provenance. A successfully persisted Payment outcome must be retained if Reservation confirmation subsequently fails.
- **Conditions:** A verified initial partial payment may support confirmation when it satisfies the historically agreed initial payment requirement and all approved confirmation conditions. A completed initial Payment must not be represented as full Reservation settlement where a contractual future balance remains. Reservation specification owners must define permitted predecessor states, fulfilment and operational prerequisites, the exact confirmation operation, discrepancy and concurrency handling, and treatment of multiple or conflicting verified outcomes. Do not reuse the incompatible legacy confirmation operation without explicit specification reconciliation.
- **Confirmation:** APP-004.1/004.2 operation, policy and snapshot amendments; SPEC-026 confirmation clarification, SPEC-027/028 provenance review and SPEC-029 conditional-save contract.

### D10 — How is the historical Reservation's first Payment established and recovered?

- **Question:** How should the existing Reservation's initial Payment be created or recovered if no PaymentAttempt exists?
- **Evidence and constraint:** AR-003.1 found no matching candidate in a bounded historical query; that observation is not current state. An unattempted Payment may be valid. [AR-003.3 §§2.1, 7.2-7.3](AR-003.3-payment-aggregate-persistence-and-itn-reconciliation-design.md#73-historical-reservation)
- **Architect recommendation:** After a fresh authorised read, create or reuse Payment for the existing Reservation before creating an attempt; compare immutable identity, snapshots, exact amount, currency and method, and recover a matching unattempted Payment.
- **Alternatives:** Create a new Reservation; infer a historical Payment. Both are rejected as unsafe or outside this task's scope.
- **Consequences:** No automatic deletion or replacement; collisions, stale pricing or inconsistent context require review. Initiation recovery and access are separate decisions.
- **Project Owner decision:** **PENDING**.
- **Confirmation:** SPEC-026/027/028/029 against approved D01-D06 and migration; D11 access authority separately.

### D11 — What permits customer access?

- **Question:** What established authority permits customer-facing initiation and access to Payment, return and confirmation information for the specific Reservation?
- **Evidence and constraint:** Journey selection and an anonymous session alone do not establish Reservation authority. Provider ITNs are server-to-server and independent of browser access. [AR-003.3 §§2.3, 10](AR-003.3-payment-aggregate-persistence-and-itn-reconciliation-design.md#10-decision-register)

- **Alternative:** Enable on Journey selection alone, which AR-003.3 does not recommend.
- **Consequences:** Customer-facing access remains gated; this review does not choose or design an ownership mechanism.
- **Project Owner decision:** **PENDING**.
- **Confirmation:** Separate access/security contract review; SPEC-026/027/028/029 confirmation unless that review identifies an actual required change.

### B01 — Dynamic, tour-specific and component-derived payment requirements

- **Project Owner decision:** **APPROVED**, 2026-11-17. Applicable tour and package business logic dynamically determines the amount payable at booking and its commercial basis for the individual Reservation. The only permitted outcomes are `PARTIAL_PAYMENT` and `FULL_PAYMENT`; a zero-payment outcome is prohibited. Neither a universal fixed deposit nor a universal percentage or a hardcoded day-tour/package distinction determines the result.
- **Private day tours:** The current standard is a configurable 50% deposit when booked more than seven days before the tour, with the balance due seven days before the tour; booking seven days or fewer before the tour, including exactly seven days, requires full payment at booking. Fifty percent is not an architectural constant.
- **Packages:** Actual selected components and applicable commercial and supplier conditions determine the requirement; no universal package percentage or amount is prescribed. Reservation preserves the agreed requirement, basis and relevant deadlines historically; subsequent rule or supplier changes do not silently change it. Commercial changes require an explicit amendment process.
- **Boundaries:** Tour and package business logic determines the requirement; Reservation preserves agreed terms; Payment represents an individual transaction and does not calculate the obligation. Application orchestration coordinates them. A completed Payment does not by itself establish full Reservation settlement. Later balance collection, invoicing, reminders, accounts receivable and manual-payment reconciliation remain outside this scope; the initial online transaction still requires verification and reconciliation.
- **Consequences and confirmation:** This business decision supersedes AR-003.3 §9.1's initial full-payment-only recommendations as a limitation on the intended business scope, not the existing approved specifications. D05 and D09 are now approved subject to their recorded conditions; the detailed calculation, confirmation and persistence contracts remain outstanding. D01 remains limited to its approved first-full-payment scope; D02 and D04 and all their conditions remain unchanged. See [AR-003.6](AR-003.6-deposit-and-full-payment-reconciliation-design.md) for controlled proposed amendments and unresolved decisions. Specification owners and architecture governance must review and approve any amendments; B01 does not authorise implementation.

### B02 — Dynamic payment requirement calculation ownership

- **Project Owner decision:** **APPROVED — Option A**, 2026-11-17. Tour/package commercial business logic is the authoritative owner of calculating the dynamic initial payment requirement, accounting for applicable commercial rules, selected package components and relevant supplier conditions.
- **Responsibilities:** APP-004.4 Reservation Payment Policy consumes and evaluates the calculated requirement within its policy responsibilities; it is not the authoritative commercial calculation engine. Reservation preserves the agreed requirement, amount payable at booking, calculation basis, applicable terms and contractual future due dates. Payment remains a separate Financial Aggregate representing an individual transaction; it does not calculate deposits, interpret supplier policies or determine whether the entire Reservation obligation is settled. Application orchestration coordinates the calculation result and Reservation and Payment responsibilities.
- **Scope and follow-up:** This ownership decision did not change B01's approved commercial rules or D01–D11 and their conditions. Detailed calculation, confirmation and persistence contracts remain outstanding; neither specification amendments nor implementation are authorised by B02. See [AR-003.6](AR-003.6-deposit-and-full-payment-reconciliation-design.md) for the draft reconciliation.

### B03 — Full collection of selected accommodation amount

- **Project Owner decision:** **APPROVED**, 2026-11-17. For every selected Hotelbeds accommodation rate, collect 100% of the selected accommodation amount at booking, whether the rate is refundable or non-refundable.
- **Conditions and separation:** Preserve the actual supplier cancellation deadline, penalty conditions and supplier payment conditions separately for each selection. Refundability and cancellation rights do not reduce the amount collected from the customer at booking. This decision does not equate GCT customer collection timing with supplier settlement timing.
- **Follow-up:** Resolve rate revalidation/freshness, currency compatibility and exact amount semantics through the calculation-contract review in [AR-003.6](AR-003.6-deposit-and-full-payment-reconciliation-design.md). No specification amendment or implementation is authorised.

### B04 — Go Cape Tours component collection timing

- **Project Owner decision:** **APPROVED**, 2026-11-17.
- **Multi-day tours and packages:** More than 14 days before departure, collect 50% of the Go Cape Tours component at booking; exactly 14 days or fewer, collect 100%. The remaining 50%, where applicable, is contractually due 14 days before departure.
- **Private day tours:** More than 7 days before the tour, collect 50% of the Go Cape Tours component; exactly 7 days or fewer, collect 100%. The remaining 50%, where applicable, is contractually due 7 days before the tour.
- **Conditions:** Both 50% values are configurable commercial policy, not architectural constants. These rules apply to the Go Cape Tours component; the selected accommodation component follows B03. B04 does not resolve calendar/timezone interpretation, currency, precision or rounding, calculation source/version, or amendment handling.
- **Follow-up:** Detailed calculation contract and dependent specification-owner review are documented in [AR-003.6](AR-003.6-deposit-and-full-payment-reconciliation-design.md). No specification amendment or implementation is authorised.

- A parallel Payment table or a general one-Payment-per-Reservation cardinality. A parallel table would require explicit authority and must not violate the cited PayFast specification's ban on a parallel persistence architecture; the cardinality is not established by existing authority.
- Fabricated intermediate financial events, amounts or timestamps; verified provider completion alone does not establish those stages.
- Cross-Aggregate database transactions. In particular, SPEC-029 §29 excludes unrelated Aggregate Roots from the Reservation repository transaction; any different transaction direction requires explicit architectural reconciliation rather than an assumed exemption.
- Assigning an unmatched verified ITN to a guessed Payment or Reservation, or customer-facing Payment access before authority and security are established.
- Any implementation, schema or migration change, database operation, provider request or payment execution. Approved directions remain subject to their conditions and controlled approvals; unselected alternatives remain proposals.
